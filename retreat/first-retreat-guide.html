Beginners Welcome

Your first Osho meditation retreat

You do not need previous meditation experience, special flexibility, or a particular belief. Curiosity and a willingness to participate are enough.

What to Expect

Active practice followed by quiet.

Many Osho meditations begin with breathing, movement, sound, shaking, or dance and then move into stillness. Instructions are offered before a session. You can ask questions and adjust your participation when needed.

The weekend also includes shared meals, talks, workshops, music, nature, and unstructured time. The purpose is direct experience rather than belief or performance.

Your pace mattersYou are responsible for choosing the intensity that is appropriate for you. Tell a facilitator about an injury, health consideration, or access need before a practice begins.

Packing List

Bring comfort, layers, and a little room to breathe.

For meditation

  • Loose, comfortable clothing
  • Extra layers and socks
  • Water bottle
  • Small towel
  • Any personal sitting support

For the mountains

  • Walking shoes
  • Rain layer
  • Warm evening layer
  • Flashlight or headlamp
  • Personal toiletries and medicines

Optional

  • Journal and pen
  • Red or maroon daytime clothing
  • White clothing for evening meditation
  • Earplugs or sleep mask
  • A musical instrument for shared time

A Typical Day

Energy rises and settles.

Morning

The day begins early with Dynamic Meditation, followed by breakfast and a morning talk or inquiry.

Afternoon

Lunch, rest, an experiential workshop, and Kundalini Meditation create space for both learning and release.

Evening

Dinner leads into White Robe Meditation, music, or another shared program, followed by quiet hours.

Community Care

Presence includes respect for one another.

  • Ask before photographing or recording another participant.
  • Keep personal stories shared in group spaces private.
  • Respect quiet hours and designated silent periods.
  • Use fragrance lightly and share dietary or access needs in advance.
  • Ask a facilitator when you are unsure about a practice.

Questions before deciding?

Write to the retreat team. A practical conversation is often the easiest way to know whether the gathering fits your needs.
